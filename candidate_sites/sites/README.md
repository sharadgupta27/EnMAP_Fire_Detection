# Per-site shapefiles

Generated by `../make_site_shapefiles.py` from `../sites_ordering.csv` and
`../enmap_scenes.csv`. All layers are WGS84 lon/lat (EPSG:4326).

Two layers per candidate site:

| file | contents |
|---|---|
| `W04_Indochina_NEIndia_DT0000120123_t018_AOI` | **Myanmar, 2025-03-23** - the only tile with a fire burning at the EnMAP overpass |
| `ALL_wildfire_candidates` | **all 10** evaluated tiles, accepted and rejected, each with its verdict |

Only tiles whose verdict begins `WILDFIRE` get their own AOI file, because
an AOI is something you upload to a search portal and a rejected tile must
not be orderable by accident. `ALL_wildfire_candidates` is the audit trail
instead: it holds every tile that was considered together with the reason
nine of the ten were not chosen, which is what a reviewer will ask for and
what a folder of accepted AOIs cannot answer. Do not upload it as a search
AOI - several portals silently use only its first feature.

Both layers carry the same 27 attributes (single-part polygons,
EPSG:4326), so the evidence travels with the geometry.

### Correction, 2026-09-08 - W01/W03 Colorado withdrawn

The first version of this search recommended `W01` (Colorado Rockies,
2026-08-20). **It has no fire at the EnMAP overpass** and its AOI files have
been deleted. Two defects caused it:

* **The active-fire test was never contemporaneous.** Sentinel-2 revisits
  every 5 days, so its fire pixels almost never coincide with EnMAP. The
  search took the S2 scene with the *most* fire pixels within +/-3 days and
  recorded no time offset for it, so W01's "34 active-fire pixels" turned
  out to come from `S2B_13TCE_20260818`, **48.6 h before** EnMAP and under
  27 % cloud. `max_sensor_dt_h` measured EnMAP/Landsat/ECOSTRESS only and
  never included Sentinel-2, so nothing flagged the gap.
* **The one concurrent measurement was ignored.** ECOSTRESS is constrained
  to a few hours of EnMAP, so it is the only partner that can say what was
  burning at the overpass - and its LST was already in the record. It was
  used purely as a data-availability check (`valid_px`) and its temperatures
  were never read. W01's ECOSTRESS reads **max 320.6 K against a 301.8 K
  background** - a hot summer hillside, no anomaly at all. For contrast, the
  Palisades fire reads 398.6 K.

W01's 21.6 % "burn scar" was also largely pre-existing: the same tile
already read 13.4 % burn scar five weeks earlier, consistent with the older
fire scars across that part of the Front Range. NBR cannot date a scar.

`classify()` now rejects any tile with no ECOSTRESS thermal anomaly at the
overpass (`hot_px`, pixels above `max(330 K, tile median + 15 K)`), ranks by
that anomaly rather than by the stale Sentinel-2 count, and records
`s2_dt_h` so non-contemporaneous evidence is visible. Re-running the same
10 candidates under the corrected test leaves **one** survivor, `W04`.

**The shortlist itself is still suspect.** These 10 tiles were selected into
the search by the same broken Sentinel-2 criterion, so the region-wide
search should be re-run with the thermal-anomaly test as the primary filter
before `W04` is treated as the best available site rather than merely the
best of a compromised shortlist.


## Withdrawal, 2026-09-08 - S3, S6 and S7 removed

`retest_legacy_sites.py` re-measured every `S*` tile with the corrected fire
test - nearest Sentinel-2 pass at <=40 % cloud within +/-5 d, offset recorded -
so that the old sites and the new `M*` sites are ranked by the same
measurement. Three sites have **no tile that reaches either threshold**
(burn >= 5 % or >= 50 active-fire px), and their AOI and scenes layers have
been deleted:

| site | what the retest found |
|---|---|
| `S3` Vojvodina / S Hungary | max burn **2.7 %**, 34 fire px over 4 tiles. Crop-residue burning: a handful of field fires, nothing with a radiant signal to retrieve. |
| `S6` Mojave / Victorville | max burn **2.1 %**, 41 fire px on its single tile. |
| `S7` Galicia / N Portugal | max burn **1.6 %**, 93 fire px over 8 tiles - and every Sentinel-2 partner is 24-48 h from the overpass, the same non-contemporaneous evidence that sank W01. `../README.md` already called S7 "the compromise". Superseded by `M1` and `M3`, which cover the same August-2025 Iberian fires with 3,066 and 71 fire px at 0 % cloud. |

Their rows stay in `../sites_ordering.csv` and `../enmap_scenes.csv` as the
audit trail, and the per-tile numbers are in `../legacy_sites_retest.csv`.
What is suppressed is the AOI: `make_site_shapefiles.py` now carries a
`WITHDRAWN` set and skips them, so re-running it does not resurrect them.

The seven surviving `S*` sites all have real fire. `S1` Yalu is the strongest
(19 of 25 tiles on fire, 2,969 fire px, contemporaneous to 0.5 h).

## The megafire sites (`M*`), 2026-09-08

Generated by `../make_megafire_shapefiles.py` from
`../megafire_enmap_verified.csv`. Same conventions as the `S*` layers: WGS84
lon/lat, one single-part polygon per AOI. See `../MEGAFIRES_POST2023.md` for
how the sites were found and what each one is.

These answer a different question from the `S*` and `W*` sites. Those were
selected for sensor coincidence and found mostly small fires - crop-residue
burning, a few hundred Sentinel-2 fire pixels. The `M*` sites start from the
largest individual wildfires worldwide since 2024 and ask whether EnMAP flew
each perimeter.

| file | contents |
|---|---|
| `M<n>_<name>_AOI` (+`.zip`) | One search polygon per site - the envelope of the tiles that have the fire in them, not the whole datatake strip. 23 attributes carry the evidence. |
| `M<n>_<name>_tiles` | That site's EnMAP tile footprints, each with its own `fire_px`, `burn_pct` and `onfire` flag. |
| `ALL_megafire_sites` | All 110 tiles across all 16 sites, accepted and rejected. The audit trail - do not upload it as an AOI. |

The AOI is the on-fire envelope rather than the datatake extent because a
megafire datatake is a ~500 km strip of which two or three tiles are over the
burn; ordering the strip is not the intent.

**Tier 3 sites get no AOI file**, only rows in `ALL_megafire_sites`: `M14`
Sierra Oeste (largest Spanish fire ever, but EnMAP flew either side of the
perimeter), `M15` Uiseong-Andong (largest Korean fire ever, strip sits east
of the burn) and `M16` Pantanal (no EnMAP in the fire window). This follows
the same rule that kept the rejected `W*` tiles out of the AOI set: an AOI is
something you upload to a portal, so a site that should not be ordered must
not be sitting in the folder as an orderable polygon.

Tier 1 is `M1`-`M6` (fire burning under EnMAP, cloud-free); tier 2 is
`M7`-`M13` (fresh scar rather than flame).

## Regenerating

All three builders need GDAL, so run them with the `emit` interpreter
(`conda` is not on PATH - call the executable directly):

    E=C:/Users/shara/miniforge3/envs/emit/python.exe

    $E make_site_shapefiles.py                       # the S* sites
    $E wildfire_4sensor.py --json <pertile_pass4.json>    # the W* sites
    $E make_megafire_shapefiles.py                   # the M* sites

from the parent directory. Each rewrites its layers from the CSVs, so edit
the CSVs rather than the shapefiles.

Note that `megafire_verify.py` and `retest_legacy_sites.py`, which produce
those CSVs, currently **cannot** run under `emit`: both `rasterio` and
`osgeo.gdal` in that environment hang indefinitely opening the Sentinel-2
COGs on `sentinel-cogs.s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com`, with no error raised.
The same reads work under the system `C:/Python314/python.exe`, which is
what generated the CSVs. Worth fixing in the environment.
